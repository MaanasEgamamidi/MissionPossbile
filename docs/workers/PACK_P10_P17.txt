Science is shared across these specialties. Any character may read general instruments, allocate resources, carry supplies, clean accessible solar panels, drive a built rover, and operate ordinary charging controls. Mark specialist interventions with a symbol, text, portrait, and appropriate tool. Do not make an entire facility unusable to other players.
Four characters arrive in every mode. One to four human players can join from the beginning. With fewer than four players, a player physically at the main hub may select an unoccupied character and take control at that character's current location. The released character stays at its designated hub spot. With four players, switching is disabled. Host authority prevents two peers selecting the same character.
Inactive characters wait at protected hub spots, consume no resources, and retain frozen personal condition. Equipment, cargo, health, stamina, and suit charge belong to each character and remain with that character when control changes. Switching cannot move cargo across the world, heal damage, recharge a tank, or duplicate equipment. No parked character performs autonomous work.
[heading 1] Time survival and session rules
Use a 20-real-minute sol by default and provide a clearly labeled host setting for sol duration. An implementation default range of 5 to 120 minutes is acceptable. This is a game-time control, not an alteration of the scientific length of a Martian day. Preserve the current sol phase when the setting changes, and replicate the new value to clients.
Pause the simulation in solo menus. In co-op, pause only after unanimous agreement; opening one player's panel does not pause everyone. A closed save has no offline deterioration. Movement, animations, the one-second suit check, real network latency, and the intentional radio delay do not scale with sol duration. Keep voice available while the simulation is paused.
Use Mars gravity throughout the stationary base and exterior. The supplied NASA fact sheet lists mean surface gravity as 3.73 m/s2; use that documented reference and distinguish other latitude-specific values if introduced. Tune jump impulse and locomotion under this gravity rather than retaining the old Earth-gravity jump unchanged. [N01]
On condition reaching zero, recover the astronaut at the hub. Keep the worn suit and signature tools. Put ordinary carried cargo in one persistent, recoverable cache at the incident location. Charge 0.1 sol of one active astronaut's food and drinking-water allowance, taking only available supplies and never blocking recovery. Display the cost and cache location. Restore a playable condition without silently refilling portable tanks; powered life support and charging still matter.
A client connection loss returns its character and inventory safely to the hub without a cargo drop or recovery charge. Release the slot and support reconnection to an available character. This courtesy is an explicit exception to the ordinary incident penalty. Host loss returns clients to the menu; the host owns the shared save and can reload it. Automatic host migration is excluded.

[heading 1] Learning and facility progression
Use one progression framework across sectors. An upgrade becomes eligible through the required knowledge answers, successful practical tasks, demonstrated competence, and the relevant current tier. Being able to pay for an upgrade is necessary when it consumes parts, but payment alone does not establish competence. Repeating a memorized answer must not produce unlimited progress.
Represent upgrades as branching cards. Each card names its function, required tier, knowledge checks, practical evidence, materials, responsible specialist, installation location, and resulting equipment change. The player sees why a card is locked and can choose among the eligible cards. Tiers summarize earned competence in that sector; do not impose a single fixed purchase sequence on all facilities.
Implementation default: tier 0 is unfamiliar; tier 1 is earned by completing a guided task and its explanation check; tier 2 requires independent diagnosis in a changed example; tier 3 requires a controlled comparison or successful transfer to a new operating condition. These tier labels are implementation defaults for the approved learning mechanism, not a newly approved large skill tree. Knowledge and competence are shared colony progress so a returning co-op player does not force everyone to repeat a lecture.
Use short, contextual questions with explanations and retry variants. Give feedback that connects an answer to the actual equipment. Do not deduct health for a wrong knowledge answer or grant mastery for answer count alone. At a terminal, show locked requirements, eligible choices, expected resource effects, and the installation action separately.

    | Food || Irrigation reliability; nutrient metering; scheduled lighting/watering. Evidence: compare a plant symptom with measurements, adjust one cause, and observe growth response.

Hold additional upgrade ideas outside the build. Gravity is the same in all facilities. Habitat pressure, safe oxygen, and dust exclusion are common physical constraints; equipment demands differ. Greenhouse nitrogen belongs to crop nutrition and fertilizer management. Do not automatically enrich its air with nitrogen or prescribe one fertilizer recipe for every crop. [N07, N08]
[heading 2] Two oxygen production paths
Keep water electrolysis and atmospheric CO2-to-oxygen processing as distinct equipment paths. Electrolysis consumes suitable water and electricity. MOXIE-inspired processing takes atmospheric CO2 and energy/heat. Both need usable equipment and oxygen storage/quality handling. CO2 removal from occupied cabin air is a separate function. Neither an oxygen gauge nor an oxygen generator alone proves adequate habitat pressure. [N04, N05]
NASA demonstrated these mechanisms in different settings and at different scales. Use separate source labels for ISS life support, the Mars MOXIE experiment, and the fictional 2080 colony installations. The game may simplify heat, byproducts, rates, and maintenance, but must label that simplification rather than attribute its balance numbers to NASA.

[heading 2] Rover construction and progression
Build one configurable four-seat rover using the approved chassis. Four seats remain available in every configuration. Sliders control five stats: speed, motor torque, battery range, cargo capacity, and traction. Show the current and projected stats, part cost, battery state, and effects of actual cargo load. Offer custom configurations; examples may help explain the system, but presets cannot replace the sliders.
Each stat has levels 1 through 5. Level 1 is the base equipment. Start with 10 usable parts. The four upgrade steps cost 1, 2, 3, and 4 parts respectively, so fully upgrading one stat costs 10 parts and all five costs 50. These are approved tunable game-balance values. More salvage eventually permits every stat to reach level 5; there is no permanent chassis-point cap preventing that outcome.
Only the field engineer/geologist assembles or refits the rover at the garage. The assembly challenge covers selecting compatible modules, placing/connecting them, and verifying the resulting power/load behavior. Avoid rapid-input tests and repeated identical puzzles. Any character may gather/transport salvage, drive, ride, and use normal charging controls. Recover and assess existing hardware, then refurbish compatible parts; do not imply raw rock instantly becomes a motor.
Refitting fully reuses invested parts. Preserve stored battery energy rather than refilling the battery when its capacity changes, enforce cargo capacity, and prevent duplicate refunds. Collect enough recoverable world parts for rover progression and required facility improvements without requiring all rover stats to be maximized for campaign completion. Exact part locations must come from approved objects in the supplied map.
[heading 2] Communications
Indoors, proximity speech is immediate, full volume through 5 m, and fades to silence by 25 m. Account for room/door separation. If either speaker or listener is outside, use the radio across the playable map, including indoor recipients. The five stages are no link, 30-second delay, 15 seconds, 5 seconds, and near-immediate. Install upgrades at the base communications station through the shared knowledge/task framework.
The intentional delay represents damaged equipment buffering/processing. Keep it separate from network jitter and from Earth-Mars light travel time. Upgrading local radio does not reduce Earth distance. No separate always-available team channel is approved. Voice stays in the private session and temporary buffers; do not store recordings. [N06]
[heading 2] Weather and replayability
Ship a verified NASA archive subset and continue offline with the last validated data. Check for published updates at startup and hourly while online; the background refresh is required. Cache updates atomically and report their observation date and validation status. Do not call archived observations live weather or a forecast for the fictional colony. [N02, N03]
Use saved seeds and observation-derived episode variation for timing, duration, intensity, dust, and solar effects. Preserve relationships among variables and distinguish observation time from game time. Start with a minor teaching event after the player can respond. Escalate within recoverable limits using readiness, warning, and reserves. Avoid fixed disaster intervals and automatic catastrophic first storms. Different seeds should support varied runs; do not promise mathematical uniqueness for every pair of runs.
[heading 2] Campaign and story
Aim for a first sustainable-colony milestone in two to three hours. Complete the tutorial, restore all five sectors and communications, and sustain essential operation with positive reserves for a full sol. Continue in the same world afterward. The optional mystery investigates a previous power/reserve/distribution cascade through short records and environmental evidence. Solving it is not a completion requirement. Keep the tone hopeful and non-graphic, with no new NPC inhabitants or cutscenes.
