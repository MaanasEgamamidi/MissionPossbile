## Ownership and dependency plan

The parent integrator owns Git, F06 world data/spawn/integration and final verification. The orchestrator owns this audit, execution ledger and contract review. The foundation specialist owns F02-F04 state/actions/session/save/replication contracts and their tests. The UI specialist owns F05 shared shell, sample and UI checks. Three child slots are the session maximum; the user's request for five concurrent subagents cannot be met by this runtime. Contracts are exchanged directly before integration.

Order: F01 audit -> F02 stable shared contract -> F03 roles and F05/F06 integration -> F04 persistence/replication verification. No dependent feature should invent a second task schema.

## Risks and decisions

- The foundation must fail closed while actual puzzle validators are absent. A dummy UI demonstration cannot award colony repairs or progression.
- Operator claims must be transient, host-owned and invalidated by death, disconnect, crew switch, timeout, stale generation or loss of reach. Distinct tasks in a room permit independent operators.
- First completion, current operational condition and seen-shot notification records remain distinct, so later maintenance does not erase narrative achievement or duplicate a payoff.
- Save migration is additive and defaults missing records. Real saves are never used as test fixtures. Exposure elapsed time remains durable through reload; no offline time is added.
- Role/exposure validation cannot rely solely on a client-provided room, role or completion value. Safe crew switching cannot refill or heal the character being left behind.
- F06 adds future cassette anchors only. Activating them should state that the cassette feature is not implemented in this foundation scope.
- Human feel, real two-computer LAN and art approval cannot be inferred from headless assertions. Mark any checks not actually performed explicitly in `HANDOFF.md`.
